Where I stand on trees

Our big trees took generations to grow. I want rules that are honest, fair to homeowners, and actually work.

Gulf Breeze has been a Tree City USA for 33 years as of 2024 (Florida Urban Forestry Council). When I read our tree code line by line, I found some gaps. Here is what I found, what I would do, and what I can’t promise.

What the rules say today

  • Protected trees. Trees 12 inches and up need a permit to remove. The city’s 2022 tree ordinance sets those rules. Normal permit rules came back Oct 26; tree permits are still free (City of Gulf Breeze press release, Oct 21, 2026; FY2027 adopted fee schedule).
  • The fine. Tree violations in Gulf Breeze are handled by city citation: $250 for a first violation and $500 after that (City Code Sec. 22-197). No code enforcement board or special magistrate since 2002 (Gulf Breeze Ord. 07-02, 2002; City Code Secs. 2-151 to 2-159).
  • Heritage trees (48 inches and up). Same $250 / $500 fine. Sec. 22-170 says removal must be mitigated “as set forth below.” Nothing is written below.
  • Tree mitigation money. The city expects about $24,000 in tree mitigation fees this year, but budgets just $500 a year to spend on tree mitigation (FY26 budget packet). Today that money goes into the stormwater fund. When I spoke with city staff, they told me tree mitigation money isn't spent on stormwater work or staff salaries. I appreciate that. But the city's audit doesn't show it as a separate tree reserve, so I want a dedicated tree fund line item with public reporting.
  • What we don’t have. Gulf Breeze had 50% tree canopy (Florida Forest Service, 2021 data, published Dec 2023), but we have no canopy goal. No setback break to save a big tree.

Sources: City of Gulf Breeze FY2027 budget workshop packet, Jul 20, 2026, pp. 227–230 (revenue line 201-0000-343.70-05; expense line 201-0531-552.52-04); Ord. 03-2022 (tree protection, adopted Aug 15, 2022).

What state law says

  • Dangerous trees. Since 2019 (tightened in 2022) (F.S. 163.045; HB 1159, 2019; SB 518, 2022), the owner of a single-family home can remove a tree with a signed, onsite risk assessment, done the ISA way, that finds it an unacceptable risk. No city permit needed. That is legal under state law, and I respect it.

What I would do

  1. Setback relief to save a big tree. Let a builder shift a house a few feet instead of cutting a big oak. Winter Park allows up to 10 feet (Winter Park Code Sec. 58-283(e)). Coral Gables allows up to 20% (Coral Gables Zoning Code Sec. 82-33). This loosens rules.
  2. Tree money for trees, and voluntary programs. Put tree mitigation money in a dedicated tree fund line item for replanting, with public reporting. Use public-tree programs and state forestry grants, and let owners volunteer their big trees as heritage trees. Fernandina Beach lets owners register native trees 36 inches and up, and its city tree fund pays 25% of pruning and all checkups (Fernandina Beach LDC Sec. 4.05.15).
  3. Fix the heritage gap. Write the missing heritage mitigation formula.

What this means for you as a homeowner

  • Need to remove a protected tree? Ask the city for a permit.
  • Think a tree is dangerous? Get a signed, onsite ISA-method risk assessment before you cut.
  • Check the signer’s credential: an ISA-certified arborist or a Florida-licensed landscape architect.
